Independent interactive installation

TEXTILE2050

One industry.
Two possible futures.

What might textile production leave behind? I built two speculative worlds and connected them to everyday materials: a strip of cloth and a dye bottle. Visitors handle the objects to change the projected animations.

My role
Independent project: concept, 3D scene development, animation, Arduino / TouchDesigner interaction and installation.
Medium
Two physical inputs, two projected worlds. Blender, Cinema 4D, Arduino and TouchDesigner.
See the two interactionsInside the systemWatch the animation

The futures are a contrast,
not a forecast.

“Extinction Express” imagines textile production in an exhausted landscape. “Post Anthropocene” places an alternative production system among living plants. The contrast turns an environmental question into two spaces a visitor can encounter.

Extinction Express 毁灭号

Heavy machinery, hanging cloth and a depleted environment frame the destructive future.

Post Anthropocene 理想国

A transparent machine, dye elements and a planted landscape frame the alternative.

Two materials.
Two ways into the story.

The interaction starts with something tangible. Each object belongs to textile production, so the physical action is part of the subject rather than a separate controller.

Pull the cloth

Extinction Express · pressure-sensor input

  1. Handle the cloth

    A visitor pulls fabric at the black-box station.

  2. Register the event

    The pressure-sensor circuit supplies an input event to Arduino.

  3. Change the scene

    TouchDesigner switches the animation from moving cloth to a rising forest of steel forms.

Lift the dye bottle

Post Anthropocene · distance-sensor input

  1. Pick up a bottle

    The visitor moves a dye bottle placed in front of the sensor.

  2. Read a distance change

    An ultrasonic sensor sends distance values through Arduino.

  3. Trigger production

    The animation shows a mechanical claw moving a dye element, followed by a packaged garment.

The clips show the real exhibition. The steps describe the interaction mapping in the original design materials; they are not a new hardware test.

From sensor data
to a change on the wall

I connected the physical inputs through Arduino serial communication to TouchDesigner. The two routes use different signals: an event count for the cloth and a distance reading for the bottle.

Event-driven switching

The preserved Arduino code reads digital pin 7 and sends an incrementing event count at 9600 baud. This is a trigger, not a continuous measurement of pulling force.

Distance-driven switching

The second circuit uses trigger and echo pins to calculate distance. The value is sent over serial to the visual system, where it controls the animation state.

Building the worlds
in motion

I developed the scenes through Blender modelling and asset refinement, then Cinema 4D composition, lighting and rendering. The animation gives each machine its own material character and production sequence.

Material on the table.
Consequence on the wall.

Fabric, bottles, visible electronics and paired projections bring the speculative scenes into a shared physical setting. The exhibition documentation shows how these elements were arranged and encountered.

What I would investigate next

The installation makes two alternatives tangible; its documentation does not establish a change in environmental knowledge or behaviour. A next evaluation would ask whether visitors notice the mapping between their actions and the animations, and how they interpret the contrast between the two futures.
