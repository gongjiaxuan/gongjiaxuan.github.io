3D motion and virtual display study

Matrix Creation

Cultural forms,
assembled from particles.

Vessels, figures and bird-like forms become fields of moving points. The film studies how recognisable cultural imagery can persist through motion, then places those forms within an imagined landscape of digital displays.

Project scope
3D particle animation, visual composition and a virtual display environment.
Presentation
Rendered film, selected particle-form studies and a virtual multi-screen landscape.
Watch the motionInspect the formsSee the display concept

A form can stay legible
while its surface changes.

Motion shifts the density and arrangement of the points. The first part of the film places these animated forms in a display setting; the later sequence isolates them against black for closer inspection.

From silhouette
to a moving field

These frames preserve three different kinds of recognition: the outline of a vessel, the sweep of a bird-like figure, and the contour of an ornament. The three sequences reveal how different contours respond to the same point-based visual language.

The screen is part
of the composition

The virtual layouts change the viewing conditions: upright panels fragment an image, while angled screens create a sequence of separate objects. The surrounding landscape gives the luminous forms contrast and scale.

What the motion study explores

The work explores recognition, fragmentation and the visual presence of cultural forms. A next study could compare how viewers recognise the same form across particle densities, viewing distances and display arrangements.

Sources: the original project statement and Matrix Creation film. The statement identifies traditional cultural symbols and artefact forms as visual references.
