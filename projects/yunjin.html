Collaborative exhibition interface prototype

Harmony with Nature

From a mulberry leaf
to a woven world.

An illustrated, wide-screen concept for exploring Nanjing Yunjin brocade. The interface connects materials and weaving with a vocabulary of motifs, then brings those motifs together in a panoramic scene.

Project context
Design collaboration: Jiaxuan Gong and 顾小漩.
Format
Adobe XD prototype for a large-screen museum interface; original artwork, screen states and process documents.
Explore the three routesWatch the prototypeFrom structure to screen

A craft can be explored
through materials or meaning.

The content structure gives visitors three related routes: the weaving process, the pattern vocabulary, and a composed world of symbolic imagery.

Making

Materials, tools and the weaving sequence.

Patterns

Individual motifs presented for horizontal browsing.

Imagery

A panoramic scene with places and figures to inspect.

Read the motif.
Then find it in the world.

Isolated silhouettes make the pattern vocabulary legible. The panoramic composition then lets the same vocabulary become a place to explore, with close-up states for individual elements.

See the wide-screen
prototype in use

The recording moves from the making process to the horizontal motif browser and then into the illustrated landscape. It shows the original interface transitions and detail views.

Organise the knowledge
before composing the landscape

The process presentation records book-based background research, information architecture and low-fidelity layout studies. These materials connect the detailed illustrations to the navigational structure.

What the interface invites

A visitor can begin with an image, a material or a making step. The next design check would be whether those entry points remain discoverable at exhibition distance, and whether small text and controls work for different visitors.

Team project credited in the original presentation to 龚珈萱 and 顾小漩. Sources: the Adobe XD prototype, screen exports, information architecture, wireframes and process presentation. Individual task allocation is not expanded beyond the available record. The project’s nature-oriented narrative is a design framing, not a measured environmental claim.
