CUT / PRINT

A small poster machine for two inks, one cut and a sheet of paper.

Why two inks

Riso printing is a stencil process. Each colour needs its own master and its own drum, so colour is rationed. Tone has to be built from halftone dots, the second colour rarely lands exactly on the first, and the paper shows through, colour and grain included.

Those limits make a good playground. There are few variables, each one shows on the sheet, and a slip in registration reads as printing. CUT / PRINT keeps the limits on screen: two inks, two screen angles, six paper stocks and registration you can loosen.

How it plays

Shuffle another version

Begin with the editable sample, or choose Start blank and pick your own lines. Each Shuffle deals a layout, a form, two inks and a paper stock. Lines you wrote or picked stay. Generated phrases can vary within the selected language. Every part of the result stays editable.

Re-cut the sheet

Choose Draw a cut and drag across the poster. The stroke becomes the new border between the ink fields, at the angle and position you draw. A light snap helps near level or upright. The largest form it crosses prints in reverse on each side.

Turn the vessel

Choose Shape the vessel, then pick two of six traditional profiles, from meiping to bowl. Drag the preview sideways to blend them, up and down to tip the form toward you. The preview follows the drag, and the poster takes the new shape when you let go. Reset form returns to the starting profile.

Ink and paper

Change the pair

Eight ink pairs, six stocks, three textures and three registration settings. The docket at the foot of the panel reports the lightness gap between the two fields, so a pairing too close to read shows up as a number.

One setting at a time

The same corner of 器物的背面 (The Other Side of Things) at export resolution, where the lip of the vase crosses the cut. Each version changes one setting from the base, clicked in the panel and exported through Print It.

Print It

The preview lays the sheet down in three passes under a moving roller: field, forms, then type. It ends with the inks, coverage and stock, and holds the export: a PNG drawn from the same state you watched being printed.

How the parts connect

Posters

Three short series. Every sheet is a Shuffle result, chosen from about 130 and exported with no further edits.

Decisions

One poster per shuffle

Shuffle builds 56 candidates from eight weighted layout recipes, scores them for balance, type clearance, colour and spacing, and keeps the best ten. It then picks at random among those that differ enough from the poster already on the sheet, since always taking the top score would keep returning the same poster.

Screens fixed to the paper

Each of the two plates has its own screen angle, 45° for the lighter field and 75° for the darker, and the dot grid is anchored to the sheet. Moving or scaling a form changes which dots it covers, and the dots themselves stay put, the way they would on a printed page.

One page, drawn twice

A browser-based canvas draws the poster. The export draws the same state again at 3,600 pixels on the long edge, about 220 pixels per inch on an A3 sheet. The file keeps the chosen aspect ratio and contains the poster without editing controls.

Limits: this is a screen simulation. The ink colours are sRGB approximations, not calibrated print predictions. The export is one flattened PNG, with no separated masters for a press.
