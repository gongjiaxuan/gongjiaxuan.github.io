Yixing Zisha AR

Exploring traditional craft through mobile AR.

My master's thesis examined how people experience AR displays of Yixing Zisha pottery. I developed a mobile prototype and studied the relationships between users' perceptions of the experience and their willingness to use it.

The prototype

The mobile application presents pottery through three areas: vessel forms, clay materials and craft processes. AR provides a way to bring digital objects and explanatory information into the user's view, rather than relying only on a static display.

The study

I combined the stimulus–organism–response framework, the Technology Acceptance Model and flow theory in a seven-construct model. I collected 388 survey responses and used covariance-based structural equation modelling to test ten hypotheses.

Findings

The analysis identified relationships between perceived authenticity, perceived usefulness and flow, informing five design recommendations for public AR displays. These are associations in the surveyed sample; learning outcomes and causal effects were not evaluated.
