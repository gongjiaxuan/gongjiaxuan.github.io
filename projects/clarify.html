Clarify

Helping people find the words—without taking the words away.

A working desktop research prototype for moments when someone knows what they need to ask but finds the conversation difficult to navigate. Clarify separates the person's intention from AI-generated suggestions, private editing, and the final decision to share a message.

01 / Problem & design judgment

Sometimes the hard part is not understanding the words. It is knowing how to ask what they mean.

“Maybe a bit later” is a normal reply. It can also leave someone unable to plan when to leave. They may know that they need a specific time but be unsure how to ask without sounding abrupt or getting drawn into another ambiguous exchange.

Clarify emerged from my research interest in generative AI and accessible communication. I wanted to explore how an interface might support people who seek help putting a question into words—including autistic adults—without presuming that AI can infer their intentions.

The design judgment was to begin with the person's stated purpose, not with a model rewriting the conversation automatically. This remains a research hypothesis, not a need or outcome established through participant study.

Fictional everyday scenario

Me

What time shall we meet tomorrow? I need to plan when to leave.

Lin

Maybe a bit later.

What I need to express

I want to confirm the time so I can plan when to leave.

01

Intent stays with the person

They define the question or purpose; AI cannot know what they meant without asking.

02

Assistance remains optional

People can type directly or use fixed phrases without generating new text.

03

Sending is its own action

A suggested sentence first enters an editable private draft, not the partner's view.

02 / Interaction mechanism

From uncertainty to a deliberate message.

The workflow separates five moments that a conventional text-rewriting button might collapse into one.

  1. 1

    Notice what needs clarifying

    Select Clarify this beneath an ambiguous message or start drafting directly.

  2. 2

    Name the intention

    Write what you want to ask. If useful, select confirmed preferences and inspect or disable conversation context.

  3. 3

    Review alternative wording

    Optional LLM suggestions appear as candidates—not as a decision about what you should say.

  4. 4

    Edit, reject, or send

    Choose a suggestion only if it helps. Edit it in the composer or keep your own words; nothing reaches the partner until Send.

  5. 5

    Hear the other side

    The partner can explain, disagree, pause or continue. Clarify does not decide the partner's meaning or assume agreement.

Why make suggestions reversible?

An apparently polished sentence can soften a boundary, add an assumption, or simply sound unlike the person who needs to speak. Clarify treats generated text as a proposal that can be inspected and changed.

The person can instead type or choose a useful phrase. The intention is the reference point; the generated sentence is not the final authority.

This is a design rationale. It does not yet establish benefit for autistic participants or communication partners.

03 / System implementation

A working Electron application with distinct private and shared states.

The architecture makes the transition from thinking to sharing explicit. A request to an AI service and a message sent to a person are different events.

Private authoring state

Intent, preferences, suggestions, draft

The writer can inspect the selected context, request alternatives, change the wording and decide what to keep.

Not displayed in the partner window.
Explicit Send→

Shared conversation state

Sent message, partner response

Only the chosen message enters the shared view. The partner can respond; a simulated partner is used for solo demonstrations.

Not a cross-device messaging service.

Electron / React / TypeScript

Desktop windows and conversation-first UI, with component states for the assistant panel, composer, partner perspective and bilingual controls.

LLM integration

On-demand wording requests through a configured OpenAI-compatible API. Inputs can include the selected message, stated goal, selected confirmed preferences and bounded conversation context.

Private / shared separation

Drafts and assistance goals remain separate from sent text. Generating or choosing a suggestion is not equivalent to publishing it.

Optional non-LLM pathways

Direct typing and fixed phrases are always available. Optional local Whisper dictation puts recognized words into the private composer for review.

A two-sided flow, not a two-device service

Alongside the writer's view, the prototype has a separate local partner-facing window. In demonstration mode, a fictional partner named Lin produces AI replies only after the writer sends a message.

This allows the interaction sequence to be examined without treating the simulated partner as a real autistic participant or a real communication partner.

Service boundary: when the online LLM is used, the selected request content is sent to the model provider. Keeping a draft private from the partner does not mean the provider receives no context. The interaction lets the person choose whether and what to request.

05 / Evidence & unverified claims

What the prototype demonstrates—and what it does not.

Implemented and checked

  • A functional Windows desktop app with private writing and local partner-facing views.
  • Working LLM request and response flows using fictional everyday situations.
  • Editable suggestions, explicit sending, simulated replies, pause and error-handling paths.
  • Interface checks at desktop and narrow widths, plus tests of optional local speech workflows.

These are engineering and demonstration checks, not studies of user benefit.

Not yet evaluated

  • No requirements study or participatory co-design with autistic adults and their chosen partners.
  • No evidence yet that the tool improves understanding, preserves meaning or reduces communication effort.
  • No verified accessibility conformance or sustained real-world use evaluation.
  • No independently authenticated, networked, two-device communication service.

These limitations shape the next research stage rather than constituting completed findings.

Next research questions

Which moments in everyday exchanges do people actually want help with? When does reviewing AI wording help—or add more work? I plan to investigate these questions through participatory inquiry and compare direct writing, fixed phrases and optional AI drafting, with attention to user control, meaning preservation and both people's workload.

06 / My contribution

Bringing a human–AI research question into an inspectable interaction.

I led the design direction and iteratively developed the functioning prototype, using AI-assisted engineering where appropriate.

Research framing
Translated questions about communication access, intent and control into a focused design hypothesis and fictional demonstration scenario.
Interaction design
Defined the sequence of clarification, user intention, optional suggestion, private revision, explicit Send and partner response.
Interface and desktop implementation
Designed and iterated the conversation UI and assistant panel, developed in Electron, React and TypeScript.
LLM and state integration
Implemented and tested on-demand model-assisted wording, selected context and preferences, and the private/shared-state boundary of the two-sided flow.
Engineering verification
Used fictional scenario walkthroughs, model-call checks, interface regression tests and explicit limitation reviews to prepare the next research iteration.

Clarify is an early, continuing research prototype. Its design and engineering checks are not claims of clinical use, user effectiveness, or a completed participant study.
